# Daily Digest: National Overhauls and Public Safety Concerns

### Educational Reforms and the Recruitment Crisis

The National Testing Agency (NTA) has initiated a significant structural overhaul following recent scrutiny of national examinations. In a move to restore institutional integrity, the NTA has removed 600 subject matter experts and replaced them with specialized newcomers. This transition is accompanied by the introduction of a rigorous four-tier question paper checking system designed to eliminate errors and leaks. Furthermore, the agency has been directed to undergo a comprehensive audit of all examination processes to ensure future transparency.

The crisis in public recruitment extends to the state level. In Jharkhand, the state government has officially released a list of 44 recruitment exams, including those for the Jharkhand Public Service Commission (JPSC) and officer posts, which have been cancelled due to various irregularities. Similarly, in Uttar Pradesh, job aspirants are mobilizing for a "Hunkar panchayat" on August 25. The protesters are demanding a transparent, time-bound recruitment process to address systemic delays. Amidst these challenges, the Odisha government has launched the "Bidesh Sikhya Bruti 2026," an overseas scholarship program for SC and ST students, with applications set to open in August 2026.

### Legislative Shifts and Administrative Accountability

The framework for the 2027 Census is taking shape, with new details emerging regarding the questionnaire. Respondents will be asked to provide detailed information, including their parents’ religion and place of birth down to the village level. In a notable shift in caste data collection, the questionnaire will offer an open field for caste declarations alongside options for "No Caste" or "Does not want to declare Caste."

On the legal and administrative front, the Enforcement Directorate and police have intensified actions against alleged corruption. Former Delhi Minister Satyendar Jain and a former CEO of the Delhi Jal Board are among six individuals arrested in connection with a suspected financial scam. In a separate development, a former Supreme Court judge has been appointed to lead an investigation into police conduct during a recent Parliament march by the Civil Justice Party (CJP). Meanwhile, the CJP has alleged that political pressure in Kolkata led to the cancellation of several of their scheduled press conferences and meetings.

In infrastructure policy, the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways is considering a new protocol where highways may undergo a "road test" under actual traffic conditions before their official public launch to ensure safety and structural integrity.

### Public Safety Risks and Criminal Investigations

A series of tragic incidents has highlighted critical gaps in public safety and social conduct. In Kolkata, nine Bangladeshi nationals were killed after a fire erupted at a local hotel at 2:00 am. Preliminary investigations suggest an air conditioner (AC) blast may have triggered the blaze, which trapped guests inside the building. This tragedy coincides with a warning from Delhi’s Chief Fire Officer, A.K. Malik, who revealed a massive manpower deficit in the capital. Malik stated that while Delhi requires over 12,000 firefighters to manage growing risks from unplanned construction, it currently operates with only 2,500.

In Madhya Pradesh, a disturbing incident at a primary school in Guna district has sparked outrage after students were allegedly forced to drink urine; the school principal reportedly told the children to "eat chocolate to feel better" afterward. In Uttar Pradesh, police arrested a woman in Pilibhit for allegedly killing a truck driver and setting his vehicle on fire, claiming the victim had been blackmailing her.

Other significant investigations include the Supreme Court seeking a government report on the disappearance of Captain Ashish Kumar, whose ship was attacked in the Strait of Hormuz. His family has reported receiving ransom calls and cryptic messages. Additionally, federal agencies are investigating a "Surat link" to the fentanyl crisis in the United States, suggesting international narcotics networks are utilizing Indian manufacturing hubs.

### Foreign Policy and Diplomatic Engagements

India has reaffirmed its long-standing diplomatic stance on the Israel-Palestine conflict. During an official visit to Palestine, Ministry of External Affairs Secretary Muktesh Pardeshi Ranganathan reiterated India’s support for a negotiated two-state solution. Ranganathan expressed India's grave concern over the deteriorating humanitarian situation in Gaza and emphasized the need for a sustainable resolution through dialogue. This engagement marks a continuation of India’s balanced approach to Middle Eastern geopolitics, focusing on humanitarian aid and regional stability.
