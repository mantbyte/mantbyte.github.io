# Mantbyte Daily Digest: Global News and World Events

### Geopolitics, Diplomacy, and Conflict
Diplomatic efforts concerning the war in Ukraine took a new turn in Moscow, where Witkoff and Kushner requested that President Vladimir Putin include Europe in upcoming peace talks. Meanwhile, in Dublin, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky stated that Europeans would join the next round of negotiations, though Russia has yet to confirm this participation. In the Middle East, ongoing conflict and UNRWA funding cuts have deepened hardships for Palestinian refugees in Lebanon. 

In regional security matters, analysts argue that the Gulf must build a security architecture that can outlast the current war, noting that recent statements from Donald Trump should accelerate the region's shift toward a layered and networked defense system. Elsewhere in Africa, Burkina Faso's state control continues to recede as rival armed groups JNIM and ISIL intensify their fight against one another. In domestic European politics, the ongoing struggle for control of Poland’s Constitutional Tribunal is actively eroding citizens' trust in the state—a development observers view as a strategic victory for Moscow.

### Domestic Policy, Governance, and Legal Affairs
In the United States, Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) reported a record number of enforcement actions in 2026, carrying out 483,200 deportations alongside numerous arrests. The scale of these actions has prompted congressional Democrats to prepare investigations into the tactics utilized. Also in Washington, the Senate Banking Committee has formally summoned former tech CEO Nvidia’s Huang and Commerce Secretary Lutnick to testify, warning that both could face legal action should they fail to appear. 

In public safety news, residents in Washington, DC, held a vigil to mourn the victims of a recent shooting that left one person dead and four others wounded. In Peru, Pope Leo met with six clergy abuse survivors, though hundreds of other victims continue to call for justice, asserting they remain excluded from such engagements. Looking ahead to the 2027 French presidential election, centrist figures are actively seeking alternatives to a polarized matchup between the far right and far left, weighing potential contenders such as judo champion Teddy Riner, while considering a bid by central banker Christine Lagarde less likely.

### Media, Culture, and Historical Retrospectives
The shifting landscape of international media and journalism is facing scrutiny from multiple angles. Al Jazeera published a retrospective examining the Global South's early efforts to challenge Western dominance in international news reporting. In Russia, an era of independent journalism has drawn to a close with the closure of one of the country's bravest newspapers, which spent over three decades covering an increasingly repressive state under Vladimir Putin.

In heritage and historical tourism, a group of seven Chinese tourists, alongside villagers and journalists, braved a muddy jungle trek using machetes to ford streams and reach the downed World War II aircraft of Japanese Admiral Isoroku Yamamoto. In maritime history, the inactive ocean liner *SS United States* has been intentionally sunk to become an undersea reef, bringing an end to years of failed rescue and restoration attempts by various owners.

### International Sports
In basketball, Detroit Pistons guard Cade Cunningham put on a historic performance in a double-overtime thriller against the Wizards, scoring 52 points and recording 10 assists without committing a single turnover—making him the first NBA player to achieve a 50-point, 10-assist turnover-free game since the 1978–79 season. In football, England secured their place in the UEFA Nations League knockouts for March after finishing second in Group A3 behind Spain, a campaign during which Harry Kane broke the country's caps record. 

Australia's international football preparations faced logistical hurdles as their upcoming friendly against Jordan, initially scheduled to be played in Amman, was moved to Qatar due to security concerns. Meanwhile, Australian cricket captain Pat Cummins publicly denied involvement in the 2018 "Sandpapergate" ball-tampering scandal, stating that teammate Usman Khawaja was "100 percent wrong" in claiming the entire team had knowledge of the incident. In tennis, Jessica Pegula advanced with a thrilling victory in the final group-one round-robin match of the WTA Finals, a result that eliminated Coco Gauff from the season-ending championships.
