# Daily Digest: Investigations, Policy Debates, and Regional Developments Across India

## Investigations and Security Operations

The Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) has made significant headway in the NEET paper leak probe, arresting 13 individuals, including three testing agency subject experts. According to the CBI chargesheet, the leak was executed through memorising questions and using chits, facilitated by notable National Testing Agency (NTA) lapses such as the absence of live CCTV monitoring and adequate frisking. 

In Jammu and Kashmir, security forces have launched a manhunt for Lashkar commander Latif Bhat, who gained notoriety following the Shopian encounter, with authorities announcing a ₹15 lakh bounty for information leading to his capture. Meanwhile, the Enforcement Directorate (ED) has raided five locations in Munger, Bihar, as part of an ongoing investigation into a digital job scam, seizing incriminating documents, bank account details, and investor lists.

## Political Developments and Policy Debates

Union Home Minister Amit Shah has assured a visiting delegation of Christian bodies that the Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Act (FCRA) Bill is "religion-neutral" and that the government has no intention of harassing the Christian community, though the forum notably raised concerns over the "deemed cessation of assets" clause. 

In southern politics, the DMK has laid down specific conditions to support the proposed delimitation Bill amid renewed efforts by the Centre to build a consensus with the opposition, though party leadership noted that formal outreach has not yet occurred. In Jammu and Kashmir, the police have filed an FIR against a PDP rally—mentioning former Chief Minister Mufti—commemorating the seventh anniversary of the abrogation of Article 370 on August 4 and August 5. The PDP condemned the action as a "blatant misuse of power and authority." 

Federal policy and infrastructure also drew attention as the Centre forwarded complaints raised by Rahul Gandhi regarding tribal displacement caused by the Ken-Betwa river linking project to the Madhya Pradesh government. In Jharkhand, a student protest entered its 14th day with an ongoing hunger strike, drawing political backing from both the BJP and Rahul Gandhi.

## Education, Welfare, and Cultural Highlights

Assam Chief Minister Himanta Biswa Sarma has launched the Nijut Moina and Nijut Babu welfare schemes aimed at reaching Gen Z youth and benefiting approximately 7 lakh students across the state. In higher education, Jawaharlal Nehru University (JNU) has opened registrations for its Diploma of Proficiency admissions for the 2026–27 session, with the merit list scheduled for August 24. Additionally, the Staff Selection Commission (SSC) is set to release the admit cards for the Hindi Translator physical test ahead of the August 17 examination.

Marking National Handloom Day, Prime Minister Narendra Modi urged citizens to purchase handloom products and share "Get Ready With Me" videos to support the nation's weavers. Cultural tributes also highlighted the enduring Ringa weaving tradition preserved by the women of Odisha’s Bonda tribe.

## Regional Updates and Environment

Agricultural and environmental concerns surfaced in Karnataka, where deficit rainfall during June and July—attributed to El Niño, geopolitical tensions in West Asia, and local factors—has left the state's rice bowl underfilled and driven up rice prices. In Telangana, property owners faced administrative hurdles as entire survey numbers containing legally purchased plots and houses were locked, preventing owners from selling, mortgaging, or transferring their properties. In wildlife-related news, a wild bear in Chhattisgarh killed a man and his sister after reacting angrily to a cub's death, continuing to linger near the victims' bodies.
