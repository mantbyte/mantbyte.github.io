# Daily Digest: Global Markets React to Tech Volatility as Security Crises Unfold Across Regions

## Markets and Technology: Nvidia, Anthropic, and the Global Economy
Global financial markets have experienced wild fluctuations as a tech-driven rally, sparked by drama at Nvidia and the record-breaking trading debut of Anthropic, partially reversed. The turmoil follows the high-profile return of Jensen Huang as CEO of Nvidia. President Trump claimed he "convinced" the company to reinstate Huang—a move that added $1 trillion to Nvidia's stock value—though the intervention has triggered political backlash and raises questions about corporate governance. 

For Beijing, Huang's return represents a mixed blessing. While China may welcome a leading U.S. advocate for artificial intelligence chip sales, analysts suggest the saga will likely reinforce Beijing’s long-term push for technological self-reliance. Meanwhile, in the Arctic, Norway has officially closed its last coal mine in Svalbard, ending more than a century of mining history as the icy archipelago transitions into a cruise ship destination and geopolitical flashpoint.

## Geopolitics, Defense, and Security Crises
NATO has vowed robust defense measures following intelligence reports that Russia plans to launch a satellite equipped with a nuclear warhead into space in the coming weeks. The warning comes amid heightened tensions in Asia, where South Korea issued an air raid alert after several Russian military jets flew near its airspace. In North Africa, Ukrainian forces are stepping up "long-range sanctions" attacks on Russian warships stationed off the coast of Libya, a development mirrored on the ground in Ukraine, where a recent Russian strike in Zaporizhzhia killed two people.

In the Middle East, Iraq’s Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi has ordered the government to take over oilfields in Kurdish-controlled areas and seize all border crossings in the region following an oil smuggling inquiry. Concurrently, Israel faces intensifying pressure on multiple fronts: Israeli air strikes in Gaza killed seven people as health officials warned of a severe meningitis outbreak, with cases surging to more than eight times the typical monthly rate. Simultaneously, reports indicate that Israel's influence war in the U.S. is faltering despite a quadrupling of its *hasbara* budget.

## Regional Conflicts and Domestic Violence
Domestic security and violent incidents have disrupted communities across several nations. In Colombia, a car bomb tore through a major city, wounding more than 20 people in the latest of a series of attacks targeting buildings housing government offices and security forces. In Vietnam, at least six people were killed and 10 others wounded following an explosion at a restaurant in Hanoi’s Dong Da district, with rescue operations currently underway. 

In Central Africa, the Democratic Republic of Congo reported a military aircraft missing after rebels claimed to have shot down a plane carrying a crew that reportedly included a South African fighter pilot and a Ugandan officer. 

## Diplomatic Shifts and Cultural Notes
In international diplomacy, Japanese leader Sanae Takaichi has broken ranks with the United States by defending the International Criminal Court (ICC). Takaichi welcomed the court’s president and vowed to accelerate financial support, defying efforts by the Trump administration to cripple the global body. 

In cultural and society news, recently published extracts from the late Pope Francis' diary reveal his claims that he saw the devil three times, including once in a choir loft during Mass. In the United Kingdom, Prince Harry and Meghan Markle have returned for a series of public appearances, prompting speculation over how their role as "nonroyal royals" will intersect with the British monarchy.
