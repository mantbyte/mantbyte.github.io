In the ancient dialogues of the *Ashtavakra Gita*, the young and deformed sage Ashtavakra offers a radical proposition to King Janaka: you are not the body, nor are you the mind; you are the all-pervading, immaculate witness of all that arises and subsides.

Imagine a vast, open sky. Storm clouds gather, lightning flashes, fierce winds howl, and torrential rains batter the earth below. To an observer caught on the ground, it may seem as though the sky itself is being torn apart, violently altered by the turmoil. Yet, the sky remains entirely untouched by the tempest. No storm can stain its blue expanse, no lightning can scar its space, and when the clouds eventually dissolve, the sky is precisely as it was before—silent, boundless, and luminous.

In our daily lives, we identify intimately with the passing weather of our minds. When anger flares, we believe we *are* angry. When anxiety clouds our thoughts, we feel consumed by the storm. We mistake the transient meteorological phenomena of our psychological states for the permanent nature of our being. We rush to fight the clouds, exhaust ourselves trying to push away the rain, and forget that we are the vast, unshakeable space in which these weather patterns simply play out.

Applying this ancient wisdom to modern life requires a subtle shift in posture. When stress, pressure, or emotional turbulence arises in the workplace or relationships, the instinctual reaction is to brace for impact or frantically attempt to fix the external environment. Instead, the teaching of Ashtavakra invites us to step back into the role of the silent witness. By pausing and asking, *“Who is aware of this anxiety?”*, we instantly create a luminous distance between our true self and the passing state. We realize that the mind can be troubled while the core of our consciousness remains completely at peace. Just as the ocean's surface churns with waves while its depths remain profoundly still, living with this awareness allows us to navigate life's inevitable storms without losing our anchor in the infinite.
