# Mantbyte Daily Digest: Key Developments Across India

### Judicial Oversight and Institutional Governance

**Supreme Court Emphasizes Standards in Elementary Teacher Education**  
The Supreme Court of India observed that high-quality elementary school educators are fundamental to childhood development and long-term nation-building. While free and compulsory education for children aged 6 to 14 has attained the status of an enforceable fundamental right, the bench expressed concern that teacher training standards have not received equivalent systemic focus or policy priority.

**Madras High Court Scrutinizes Police Station CCTV Compliance**  
The Madras High Court directed the Director General of Police (DGP) in Tamil Nadu to confirm whether every police station in the state is actively adhering to the mandate to preserve surveillance records. Presiding over the matter, Justice V. Lakshminarayanan asserted that failing to retain CCTV footage for the mandated 18-month duration—even by a minute—constitutes a direct breach of binding directives issued by both the High Court and the Supreme Court.

**Debate Over ESIC Autonomy and State Recruitment Procedures**  
A new report raised flags regarding potential privatization within the Employees’ State Insurance Corporation (ESIC). Concurrently, ESIC Director General Ashok Kumar Singh argued that implementing the Code on Social Security will expand social safety nets, allowing a broader base of workers to access benefits. In regional administrative developments, Karnataka Congress Vice-President S.A. Hussain urged the state to discontinue personality tests for Karnataka Public Service Commission (KPSC) hiring, noting that the Union Government has previously phased out interviews for Group B, C, and D positions to streamline recruitment transparency.

### Aviation Safety, Emergency Response, and Public Health

**Aviation Authorities Probe Drug Use Following Air India Incident**  
The Aircraft Accident Investigation Bureau (AAIB) submitted an interim safety report to the Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA) following a Phuket-Delhi Air India flight that suffered severe turbulence and multiple hydraulic failures. Subsequent post-flight screening in Delhi confirmed that the pilot-in-command tested positive for a psychoactive substance on two separate assessments. The AAIB underscored drug use among flight personnel as an issue of severe concern, urging the aviation regulator to institute prompt preventative interventions.

**IndiGo Ground Collision at Srinagar Airport**  
An IndiGo passenger aircraft collided with a pole during standard parking procedures at Srinagar Airport. Ground personnel reported that the impact bent the pole sideways. Authorities are examining the circumstances surrounding the tarmac maneuver.

**Evacuation Averts Tragedy at Sambhajinagar Hospital Fire**  
A fire broke out within the Neonatal Intensive Care Unit (NICU) of a hospital in Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar, Maharashtra. Emergency response personnel successfully evacuated 58 patients, including nine vulnerable newborns, without reported casualties.

**Public Health Concerns Rise Over STI Transmission in Kerala**  
Public health data from Kerala indicates a changing pattern in sexually transmitted infections (STIs) among the youth. As advances in treatment diminish the historical fear associated with HIV, shifting sexual behaviors have coincided with a documented increase in patients seeking treatment across the state's 23 care facilities for non-HIV STIs.

### Regional State Policy and Market Interventions

**Andhra Pradesh Intervenes in Commodity Prices and Land Allocation**  
Addressing inflationary pressures on consumer essentials, an Andhra Pradesh Cabinet sub-committee announced targeted market interventions, fixing onion prices at ₹35 per kilogram alongside measures covering tur dal and palm oil. The committee further directed the procurement of 10,000 metric tonnes of KNM rice from Nellore millers for subsidized distribution. In parallel administrative moves, the State Cabinet approved the reclassification of 22.31 acres of land in Nellore for housing allotments, incorporating strict oversight to prevent disruptions to the maintenance of the Sarvepalli canal.

**Kerala Examines Private University Regulations and Cybersafety**  
The Kerala government is contemplating relaxations to institutional land ownership norms and endowment fund requirements to facilitate the establishment of private universities in the state. Separately, Kerala police arrested a student for allegedly distributing digitally manipulated, explicit images of educators, with investigators indicating that multiple other youths remain under formal scrutiny in connection with the cybercrime ring.

### Political Discourse and Corporate Leadership

**Opposition Targets Central Governance on Space, Institutions, and Faith Trusts**  
Following the successful launch of India's latest space imaging satellite, Congress leader Jairam Ramesh commended the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) while concurrently accusing the Modi administration of pursuing an aggressive privatization model across both the space and nuclear energy domains. In Uttar Pradesh, state Congress Unit President Ajay Rai levied corruption allegations against the Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) and Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh (RSS) regarding the administration of the Ram Temple Trust, characterizing the recent appointment of a new Chief Executive Officer as an eyewash designed to shield financial irregularities.

**Anil Chakravarthy Named Chief Executive of Adobe**  
In global corporate leadership shifts, technology enterprise Adobe announced the appointment of Anil Chakravarthy as its new Chief Executive Officer, succeeding longtime head Shantanu Narayen. Chakravarthy, who initially joined the company in January 2020 as Executive Vice President, assumes leadership over the firm's global product and enterprise strategy.
