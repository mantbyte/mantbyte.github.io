# Daily Digest: Global News and World Events

## Geopolitics and Diplomatic Developments

High-stakes diplomacy is underway regarding the ongoing conflict in Ukraine, as US envoys Kushner and Witkoff have arrived in Moscow for direct talks with President Vladimir Putin. The meetings follow two days of trilateral negotiations in the United Arab Emirates that concluded without a breakthrough. Meanwhile, on the ground, Ukrainian civilians continue to face severe infrastructural challenges, enduring blackouts and struggling with limited access to light, water, and heat as Russian strikes target the nation's power grid. In a surreal technological fallout from the conflict, advanced defense jamming systems designed to thwart Russian airstrikes are spoofing GPS networks, causing devices across Ukraine to register their location as Lima, Peru.

In Asia, Chinese President Xi Jinping has reiterated calls for the peaceful "reunification" of Taiwan with China. In South Asia, diplomatic tensions persist surrounding Bangladesh, with statements emphasizing that former leader Sheikh Hasina must return to face her sentence, and calling on Delhi to ensure its territory is not utilized to incite violence or threaten the people of Bangladesh. In the Middle East, Iran’s chief negotiator Ghalibaf stated that Tehran remains open to a deal with the US, provided Washington refrains from "excessive demands" and actions that foster distrust. 

## Conflict and Security

In the Middle East, a deadly Israeli strike hit Nasser Hospital, the largest medical facility in southern Gaza, resulting in the deaths of at least six Palestinians and injuring 20 others. Witnesses reported that a missile struck a building housing surgical wards and intensive care units, claiming the lives of a surgeon, an official, and a disabled patient. The Israeli military stated that the target was a "senior Hamas terrorist." Inside Israel, political tensions continue to run high, with Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s ongoing fight for political survival reshaping the character of the country's democracy and turning parliamentary proceedings increasingly acrimonious.

In South Asia, a militant attack in Pakistan's northwest Kohat district killed 10 police officers and 18 fighters. The officers were ambushed while returning from a mine. Meanwhile, investigative reports reveal that India has bypassed its own regulations to acquire advanced Israeli phone-hacking technology, expanding its surveillance capabilities to include cloning phones and accessing cloud-based data from major tech companies. 

## Political Shifts and Domestic Unrest

Indonesia’s political landscape has been thrown into turmoil following the resignation of Vice President Gibran Rakabuming Raka. Gibran, whose position symbolized the ruling partnership that brought President Prabowo Subianto to power, stepped down amid a growing rift between Prabowo and former President Joko Widodo—Gibran's father—as well as ongoing scrutiny and disputes regarding his overseas academic credentials and eligibility. 

In Europe, Spain has been paralyzed by its first general strike in 14 years. Workers across a wide range of industries walked off the job to protest soaring housing costs, rising rents, and stagnant purchasing power, which led to significant street rallies and clashes with police. 

In domestic political milestones elsewhere, Democrat Scott Colom won Mississippi’s Senate race, securing his place in history as the state's first Black senator since 1874. In Africa, Ugandan police have opened an investigation into the suspected poisoning deaths of four pupils at a school, with the institution's head teacher arrested to aid the inquiry. Finally, Pope Leo was warmly received by millions during his Latin America tour in Peru—his second "homeland"—where he is slated to spend three days in Chiclayo, a city where he served as bishop from 2015 to 2023.
