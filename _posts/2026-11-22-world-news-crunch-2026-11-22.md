## Global Conflicts, Diplomacy, and Domestic Policy

South Korea’s political landscape has shifted following the resignation of Education Minister Choi Kyo-jin. The departure comes in the wake of public backlash over last-minute rules implemented just two days before the high-stakes college entrance exam. The hastily introduced measures—which aimed to stop AI-assisted cheating by enforcing loud phone alerts and eyeglass inspections—instead caused widespread chaos. 

In geopolitics, analysts suggest that US President Donald Trump’s potential exit strategy from the ongoing conflict may depend on quietly lifting the naval blockade on Iran to help wind down the war. Meanwhile, in East Africa, Somalia’s president has turned against a former ally, the United Arab Emirates, amid a domestic power struggle involving regional leaders with ties to Abu Dhabi. In Kenya, a proposed internet metering bill has sparked significant public concern regarding digital privacy, state surveillance, and rising connectivity costs. Domestically in the United States, Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) agents made headlines after masked personnel detained two individuals on a crowded airport jet bridge in Seattle as passengers boarded a flight.

## International Conflicts and Humanitarian Crises

The human cost of active conflicts continues to mount. In Ukraine, dashcam video captured the precise moment a Russian missile struck a residential neighborhood in Lviv in the western part of the country. Further south, displaced families in Darfur face a volatile winter amid deepening Sudan aid cuts, threatening to exacerbate an already severe humanitarian crisis. 

Industrial safety has also come under scrutiny following a deadly blaze at an Indonesian confectionery factory. The disaster left at least three people dead and 36 missing, with 15 others rescued and authorities reporting that the cause of the fire remains unclear. In environmental diplomacy, the UN COP31 climate summit in Turkey entered a second day of overtime as international negotiators scrambled to finalize a new draft deal aimed at averting a collapse of the talks.

## Culture, Society, and Arts

In the arts, Taiwanese comedian Huang Yi-hao is drawing sellout crowds for routines that mock Chinese leader Xi Jinping. Huang, who has dubbed Xi his "comedy muse," previously survived a car crash that authorities suspect was intended to silence him. In the world of luxury design, family representatives announced the death of Italian jeweler and businessman Paolo Bulgari at the age of 89, remembering him as "the greatest jeweller of our time." The cultural sphere also noted a growing cross-cultural debate surrounding American versus traditional Chinese mahjong, a rivalry involving differing tiles and rules that has stirred contemporary controversy and cultural misunderstandings.

## Sports Digest

Max Verstappen secured a hard-fought victory at the Formula 1 Las Vegas Grand Prix, winning the event for the third time in four years after fending off Antonelli and Russell in a race featuring 35 lead changes. In basketball, San Antonio’s Wembanyama dominated the court with a 50-point game and 19 rebounds to secure the Spurs’ 14th win of the season. 

In women's soccer, the Kansas City Current defeated the Washington Spirit 2-1 to capture their first NWSL title, driven by goals from DiBernardo and Debinha despite a valiant effort from the Spirit. In chess, the stage is set for the youngest World Chess Championship in history, where India's Gukesh is slated to defend his title against Uzbekistan’s Sindarov across up to 14 games beginning November 22 in Geneva. Meanwhile, discussions around sporting culture continue to reverberate, with fresh analyses examining what the infamous "Sandpapergate" scandal reveals about a systemic win-at-all-costs culture in Australian cricket.
