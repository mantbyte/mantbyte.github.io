# Global Markets Shaken by Tech Rout and AI Fears

Global financial markets are facing intense pressure, driven by a significant rout in tech stocks. Asian trading floors plummeted following a sharp sell-off in Western markets triggered by the US midterm elections. Investors are increasingly jittery over potential artificial intelligence curbs, evolving US AI regulations, and concerns surrounding US data centre expansion. 

Political developments in the United States continue to ripple outward. A US federal judge has ordered a 14-day pause halting construction on the controversial "Arc de Trump" triumphal arch project in Washington, DC. Meanwhile, political analysts are actively debating the long-term trajectory of American governance, questioning whether the nation can ever return to pre-Trump politics following midterms that rejected a politics of division, even as his legacy remains deeply embedded. On a diplomatic front, US First Lady Melania Trump was credited by all sides for playing an unspecified yet vital role in a ceasefire, hosting Russian and Ukrainian officials while calling for a lasting truce in Ukraine. In domestic US politics, Abdul El-Sayed achieved a historic victory in Michigan, demonstrating that Muslim Americans do not need to compromise their identity to succeed in the political arena.

# Geopolitical Tensions Grow Across the Middle East and Asia

Middle Eastern geopolitics remain fraught with multiple flashpoints. Satellite imagery from November reveals major ongoing construction, including digging and new buildings, near Iranian missile bases. Concurrently, Iran has exempted ships from India, China, Qatar, and Iraq from passing restrictions through the Strait of Hormuz, though analysts warn these localized deals carry significant political risks. In Lebanon, residents who survived past conflicts are actively packing bags and stocking up supplies amid growing fears of a new Israeli escalation. In East Jerusalem, tensions are escalating over Palestinian education, with Israeli restrictions on teachers and pressure to adopt an Israeli curriculum threatening local schools. In Israeli domestic politics, President Isaac Herzog is set to name a candidate to form a government on Sunday after a majority of lawmakers endorsed Eisenkot over Netanyahu.

In international maritime and legal disputes, a "perfect storm" has been set off by the UK’s jailing of an Indian sailor. Captain Ajay Pant was operating in international waters when his alleged "shadow" tanker was captured by British forces over Russia sanctions. In Indonesia, political intrigue is deepening as authorities named a fourth wiretapping suspect in a growing scandal that is driving divisions between President Prabowo Subianto and his predecessor, Joko Widodo. Meanwhile, in Macau, pro-democracy campaigner Au Kam San was sentenced to 12 years and six months in prison for "subversion against the political power of the state," marking the city's first national security case.

# Domestic Crises, Legal Battles, and Cultural Industry Shifts

Democratic governance and rule of law face severe tests in other regions. In Somalia, the country's top court has rejected the president's term extension amid a mounting political crisis. The decision has thrown the timeline for the nation's first nationwide elections since 1969—scheduled for next year—into serious doubt. In Europe, British families are demanding answers following a devastating summer wildfire in Bédar, Spain, which claimed 15 lives, including nine British nationals. In Greece, the influx of Israeli buyers into Athens's pro-Palestinian heartland is sparking growing local resentment.

In the cultural and sports sectors, scrutiny is falling on restrictive industry practices. The global popularity of K-Pop has been cast in a new light following a high-profile dispute between a band and its label, spotlighting the dark side of restrictive artist contracts. In sports administration and preparation, Kozo Tashima—known as a safe pair of hands with a background as a player, coach, and bureaucrat—is running for the FIFA presidency. Additionally, Cricket Australia has appointed Mike Hussey, widely known as 'Mr Cricket,' as a consultant for the Ashes, the Cricket World Cup, and the upcoming LA 2028 Olympics.
