# Daily Digest: Semiconductor Investment in Andhra Pradesh, Security Developments, and Regional Governance Updates

## Industry and Infrastructure

Andhra Pradesh is set to establish its first semiconductor plant near Visakhapatnam at Tarluvada, backed by a ₹2,387 crore investment. The chip assembly and testing unit, representing the state's inaugural project under the India Semiconductor Mission, is projected to generate 1,000 jobs. In industrial expansion elsewhere, DISA India—a subsidiary of the Danish industrial tech group Norican—has invested over ₹100 crore to expand its manufacturing facility in Tumakuru, Karnataka. The plant will produce equipment for all four of Norican's technology brands to serve both domestic and international markets. 

In regional infrastructure developments, the Kalaburagi Urban Development Authority (KUDA) has allocated ₹1.91 crore for drinking water supply. Of this funding, 65% is designated for the Kalaburagi City Corporation, while the remaining 35% will support the Rural Drinking Water and Sanitation Department. Meanwhile, Yadgir Deputy Commissioner Pandve ordered an immediate anti-encroachment drive and the formation of ward committees following public complaints regarding the encroachment of open public spaces due to missing boundary walls.

## Regional Governance and Social Welfare

In Karnataka, Koppal MP Rajashekar Hitnal distributed electric three-wheelers to eight persons with disabilities, aiming to foster financial and social self-reliance by reducing reliance on petrol transport and daily fuel costs. 

In Kerala, authorities faced mounting concern after over 50 students were hospitalized due to suspected pollution from a tar-mixing unit in Kollam. Local residents noted that the plant had resumed operations following court permission, despite local panchayat authorities having previously issued a stop memo. Politically, the LDF has accused the UDF of failing to take joint steps to address public concerns, with LDF convener T.P. Ramakrishnan claiming the state’s social life has suffered over the past six months. Additionally, Tamil Nadu BJP leader Annamalai criticized the government over delays in appointing a vice-chairman to the Cauvery technical cell, citing it as an administrative failure.

In West Bengal, the state education minister Dipak Barman announced an investigation into allegations that a Kolkata missionary school questioned a teacher for wearing a saffron saree. 

## Politics and National Developments

Uttar Pradesh Chief Minister Yogi Adityanath addressed upcoming state polls during the News18 Rising Bharat Summit, targeting the AIMIM-Chandrashekhar Aazad alliance as a "B team" of the SP and Congress. CM Adityanath also renewed his "batenge toh katenge" pitch, emphasizing historical governance issues in the state. 

Political friction emerged in Maharashtra, where Shiv Sena Shinde faction leaders tore up copies of Sonia Gandhi's memoir, accusing it of attempting to "whitewash history." Concurrently, a Delhi court sought an Action Taken Report (ATR) from police regarding a petition concerning Sonia Gandhi's pre-citizenship electoral roll entry case, with proceedings scheduled for November 28. The Supreme Court is also slated to hear a PIL filed against an Italian politician's "Vote Chor" remarks made at the Bharat Summit.

Spiritual leader Dhirendra Shastri of Bageshwar Dham addressed recent controversies, stating that his apologies were offered to prevent divisions among Hindus, while criticizing social media opposition to religious rituals.

## Security, Crime, and Public Administration

Security forces in Jammu and Kashmir celebrated a major milestone in Kulgam following the neutralization of Lateef, identified as Kashmir's last known active local terrorist. 

In Assam, Duliajan police solved a triple murder case, attributing the crime to online gaming debts. The prime accused, Raju Das, reportedly amassed debts from betting with funds borrowed from his father and enlisted an associate to kill three members of the Bhuyan family.

At the national level, the Centre issued new guidelines for district-level ombudspersons to handle grievances under the VB-G RAM G framework. The updated protocols mandate the use of geo-tagged and time-stamped photographs during field visits to bolster transparency and documentation.

In environmental updates, the Nilgiris district recorded its season's first ground frost, with temperatures dropping between 5°C and 6°C in higher elevations.
