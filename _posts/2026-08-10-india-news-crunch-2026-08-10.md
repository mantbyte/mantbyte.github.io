# Mantbyte Daily Digest: National Policy Shifts, Regional Unrest, and Diplomatic Friction

### National Governance and Legislative Developments
The framework for India’s next major administrative milestones is taking shape as the government prepares for the 2027 Census. Ladakh is set to lead the country’s first independent caste enumeration, with the census form expected to include 40 questions and, for the first time, the recording of defense structures on paper. This data will have significant constitutional implications; if Articles 81 and 82 remain unamended, the 2027 Census results will trigger a fresh delimitation process, potentially reallocating Lok Sabha constituencies across the country.

In Parliament, the 2026 Monsoon Session continues with the introduction of four key bills, including a proposal to formally rename the state of Kerala. Political maneuvering remains high as Sharad Pawar’s MPs prepare to meet Prime Minister Narendra Modi. Meanwhile, the Congress party has urged these representatives to advocate for the delinking of the women’s reservation quota from the delimitation process. In a separate legal development, the Supreme Court clarified that states possess the authority to withdraw FIRs against Citizens for Justice and Peace (CJP) protesters in accordance with the law, following the Centre's submission of its "serious" intent regarding previous assurances.

### Internal Security and Civil Unrest
Security has been significantly tightened in Jharkhand as student groups continue to protest over examination grievances. Despite government claims that 98% of student demands have been met, protesters have rejected the assertion, noting that only three of thirteen canceled exams have been addressed. The police administration has clamped prohibitory orders ahead of a proposed march to the state assembly. Leader of the Opposition Rahul Gandhi criticized Union Home Minister Amit Shah for the handling of the situation, praising the students for their "courage" in speaking out despite alleged harassment.

In northern India, preparations for the Kanwar Yatra have led to stringent local mandates. Authorities in Muzaffarnagar and Bareilly have enforced a ban on the sale of non-vegetarian food along the pilgrimage routes. In Bareilly, four individuals were detained and a shop was razed for violating these directives. Concurrently, traffic advisories and restrictions have been issued across Delhi, Uttar Pradesh, and Haryana to manage the influx of pilgrims. On the law enforcement front, police in Gujarat are conducting DNA-related forensic examinations following the arrest of a security guard for the rape of a student on a building terrace, an incident that occurred just four days after he joined the staff.

### Geopolitics and Infrastructure
Diplomatic tensions between New Delhi and Beijing have resurfaced following India’s formal naming of 27 places in Arunachal Pradesh. China criticized the move, asserting that India should avoid turning border issues into larger disputes that could "stifle" improving political ties. India maintains its sovereign right to name locations within its territory.

On the domestic infrastructure front, Chief Minister Siddaramaiah has signaled that compensation for the Bidadi township project could exceed ₹3 crore per acre. However, sources within the Greater Bengaluru Development Authority (GBDA) have characterized such high compensation rates as a "rare possibility," highlighting potential friction between political promises and agency implementation.

### Environment, Science, and Education
India’s academic and environmental sectors report significant updates this week. Delhi has already surpassed its average August rainfall within the first eight days of the month, recording 230.1 mm at the Safdarjung observatory. In the field of research, Professor Karthikeyan Lanka of IIT Bombay has been honored with the NASI Award for his work on remote sensing, drought prediction, and land surface modeling.

In education, the schedule for BITSAT 2026 counseling and seat allotment has been released, providing clarity for engineering aspirants. Similarly, Andhra Pradesh has announced the Phase 1 seat allotment results for the EAMCET 2026, marking a critical step in the state’s professional college admissions cycle.
