## Parliamentary Stalemate and Political Developments

The Indian Parliament’s monsoon session has remained unprorogued for a record 88 days as of November 9, 2026. Neither the Lok Sabha nor the Rajya Sabha has been formally prorogued, leaving open the possibility of an extended sitting. The development drew sharp criticism from the Opposition Congress party, which alleged the record was set to allow time for the government to manipulate a two-thirds majority. The party further noted that Congress president Mallikarjun Kharge has written to Prime Minister Narendra Modi four times in the past six months to request an all-party meeting. Meanwhile, discussions around local governance have emerged, with experts debating whether urban local body elections should be kept strictly non-partisan to prevent parties from unnecessarily raising the stakes and corrupting the system.

## Public Health, Infrastructure, and Agriculture

In major health developments, a new 100-bed Jayadeva Cardiac Care Centre has been proposed for Mandya, with an inspection committee scheduled to visit the district on November 12 to assess existing infrastructure and medical amenities. In Kerala, measles outbreaks are prompting a re-evaluation of disease epidemiology as the virus increasingly affects demographics outside the traditional one-to-five age group, including fully vaccinated individuals and infants. On the economic front, India’s April-August oilmeal exports fell by 28.5%, largely driven by a 73% plunge in soybean meal shipments as cheaper supplies from Brazil and Argentina captured market share, according to the Solvent Extractors’ Association of India. In urban transit, the KSRTC has introduced city-wide bus pick-up points for the first time to ease Central Business District congestion in Bengaluru and offer passengers closer boarding facilities.

## Environment, Pollution, and Urban Concerns

Air quality in the national capital deteriorated sharply over Deepavali, with pollution levels soaring to over 66 times the World Health Organization limit in parts of Delhi as residents defied a ban on firecrackers. Data during the peak pollution window on the night of November 8 and 9 was notably missing from several Delhi government monitoring stations. The Supreme Court identified vehicular emissions as a major contributor to the Delhi-NCR pollution crisis, pointing to the massive volume of traffic moving between Delhi and neighboring hubs like Ghaziabad, Greater Noida, Gurugram, and Faridabad. Concurrently, agricultural stubble burning saw a sharp spike, with Punjab recording a season-high single-day jump of 1,152 farm fires on Deepavali despite ongoing enforcement and penalties. A new study by IIM Amritsar researchers examines this persistence through the framework of 'governmentality.'

## Crime, Security, and Regional Updates

Law enforcement agencies are managing multiple high-profile criminal cases and administrative actions across states. In Bengaluru, investigators are examining how an accused individual, Indushekhar, successfully transported bodies across locations and state borders without detection by patrolling vehicles or checkpoints. In Tiruchi district, a 20-year-old youth was tragically murdered following a quarrel over bursting crackers, having been stabbed in the chest with a tester screwdriver. In Kochi, four minors were apprehended for allegedly assaulting a teen following a dispute over Instagram messages, prompting a police case filed on the victim's mother's complaint. In southern Tamil Nadu, a retired government employee was killed in a wild elephant attack in Tenkasi, triggering protests as villagers and family members briefly prevented authorities from retrieving the body. On the welfare front, the Tamil Nadu government issued formal orders granting ₹1 crore in financial aid to bereaved families of police personnel who died in the line of duty.
