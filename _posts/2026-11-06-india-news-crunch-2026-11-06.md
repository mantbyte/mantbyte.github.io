# Mantbyte Daily Digest: Key National News and Events

## Judiciary, Legal Developments, and Governance

The Supreme Court has termed vehicular emissions a major contributor to air pollution in the Delhi-NCR region, highlighting the high volume of traffic on roads connecting the capital to neighbouring areas including Ghaziabad, Greater Noida, Gurugram, and Faridabad. In a separate legal development, the apex court sought responses from NewsClick and Prabir Purkayastha regarding an Enforcement Directorate (ED) plea against a High Court order. Additionally, the Supreme Court issued a contempt notice to Kerala Congress over a controversial post concerning the Chief Justice of India, with the bench stating the matter would be dealt with an "iron hand."

Settling a decades-old energy dispute, the Supreme Court ruled that Punjab and Haryana will pay Himachal Pradesh 11,973 million units of electricity over a period of 20 years. Meanwhile, in political and electoral discourse, Trinamool Congress leader Abhishek Banerjee asserted that his party lost elections to the Supreme Court rather than the BJP. Opposition leader Rahul Gandhi criticized the central government’s stance on linking welfare schemes to Voter IDs instead of Aadhaar, terming the move a "conspiracy" and a form of "rights theft."

## Crime, Investigations, and Public Safety

A special court acquitted five individuals in a 2013 Muzaffarnagar riots case concerning the arson and vandalism of a mosque in Muthbhar village following an investigation by a Special Investigation Team (SIT). In Madurai, the Central Crime Branch registered a case under the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988, along with charges of criminal conspiracy, against former Madurai Deputy Mayor P.M. Mannan following a horse-trading complaint filed by TVK MLA Mustafa.

In Kerala, authorities issued warnings regarding an online fraud scheme targeting donors attempting to assist patients in need of blood in Kozhikode, where fraudsters solicit advance online payments for travel and disappear. On the weather and maritime front, search and rescue operations were intensified by the Tamil Nadu government and the Indian Coast Guard following Cyclone Murasu. The bodies of five fishermen from Tamil Nadu were recovered near Kothapatnam and Kothapatnam beach in Andhra Pradesh after their boat sank off the shore, while the search for remaining missing fishermen continued.

## Infrastructure, Economy, and Public Health

The Maharashtra Food and Drug Administration (FDA) issued a festive advisory outlining strict precautions for consumers and operational directions for food business operators to ensure food safety amid surging demand for sweets, khava, ghee, and dry fruits during Deepavali. In aviation, 10 smaller Gulf carriers were denied airport slots at Mumbai as the Mumbai airport prepares to suspend flights to 14 international destinations, which will be transitioned to Navi Mumbai.

To bolster economic growth, the Tamil Nadu government has constituted the Tamil Nadu Investment Promotion Commission, with the Chief Secretary serving as chairperson and the Secretary of Industries, Investment Promotion and Commerce acting as the member-convener. In public health and humanitarian efforts, the Indian NGO REACH was honored with the Kochon Prize 2026, presented by the Kochon Foundation in partnership with the Stop TB Partnership for significant contributions to combating tuberculosis. 

## Regional Affairs, Education, and Society

In Tamil Nadu, State Minister for Tamil Development, Information and Publicity Rajmohan announced that an immediate inquiry will be initiated into an alleged literary scam at the newly inaugurated Periyar Arivulagam library, flagged by Tamil writer Perumal Murugan regarding book procurement irregularities and fictitious attributions. In Jammu and Kashmir, National Conference leader Farooq Abdullah dismissed rumors of a party split, stating that the organization remains united.

In institutional milestones, Nashra Naim was elected as the first woman vice-president of the Aligarh Muslim University (AMU) students’ union in its 105-year history, during the institution's first elections in an eight-year gap where women candidates secured six of the 13 posts. Additionally, the Union Public Service Commission (UPSC) declared the results for the 2026 Civil Services Main Examination, marking another stage in the selection process for the IAS, IFS, and IPS. 

In a notable wildlife rescue operation, an 18-month-old elephant calf suffering from a genetic bone disorder—rendering it unable to stand or walk—was transported from the Kodanad elephant centre in Kerala to Gujarat’s Vantara via a specially arranged animal ambulance utilizing a crane and a team of 10 to 12 personnel for specialized treatment.
