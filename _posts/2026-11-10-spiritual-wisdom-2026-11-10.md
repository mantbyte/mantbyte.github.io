Deep within the high valleys of the ancient Zagros mountains, a weary traveler once lost his way as dusk descended. Frustrated by the creeping shadows and the winding paths that seemed to lead nowhere, he raised his voice in bitter anger and shouted into the canyon, cursing the wilderness and the cruel fate that had brought him there. To his shock, an identical voice from the deep abyss shouted back with equal fury, hurling his insults right into his face. 

Terrified by what he perceived as a hostile entity lurking in the darkness, the traveler screamed insults, threats, and fears. Naturally, the canyon faithfully threw back every harsh word, magnifying his distress. Exhausted and trembling, he finally collapsed in tears, whispering a plea for help. To his astonishment, the mountains responded with a gentle, soothing echo of compassion. He sat in the quiet dark and realized a profound truth: the canyon held no malice. It merely gave back what he cast into it.

In our modern lives, we often wander through personal and professional canyons, shouting our frustrations at colleagues, partners, and systems. When we approach the world with suspicion, defensiveness, or frantic impatience, we are met with immediate, mirrored resistance. We mistake the feedback loops of our own energy for the hostility of the external world. True wisdom begins when we stop fighting the echo and instead examine the voice.
