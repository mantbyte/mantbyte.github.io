# Global Geopolitics and Shifting Military Alliances

Strains in long-standing international alliances have come to the forefront as South Korean President Lee Jae Myung formally rejected President Trump’s request for military assistance in Iran. Despite repeated calls from Washington for South Korea to provide "a little hand" in the conflict, President Lee confirmed he will not deploy troops, marking a significant point of friction between the decades-old allies. This comes amid reports from Al Jazeera suggesting that the actual U.S. military death toll in the Iran conflict may be higher than the 18 deaths currently acknowledged by the Pentagon.

Simultaneously, the U.S. Pentagon is reportedly evaluating proposals to withdraw up to one-third of American troops currently stationed in Europe. Such a reduction would represent a fundamental shift in the Transatlantic security architecture. In the Middle East, regional stability remains precarious as Saudi Arabia issued its first air raid alerts for Riyadh since the escalation of Houthi attacks; residents were later given the all-clear following reports of explosions in the capital.

In East Asia, North Korea has dismissed a United Nations nuclear watchdog resolution, labeling the agency’s actions as "double standards" and declaring its nuclear status "irreversible." Meanwhile, in Russia, the Kremlin continues to utilize parliamentary elections as a critical mechanism for maintaining political feedback and establishing a veneer of legitimacy, moving beyond what critics often describe as mere political theater.

# U.S. Domestic Policy and Executive Mandates

President Trump has intensified his confrontation with the American media landscape by banning CNN, Politico, and MS NOW from the White House. Accusing the outlets of spreading "fake news," the move marks a new low in the administration's historically tense relationship with the press. 

The administration is also facing domestic and legal pushback on several other fronts. Protesters gathered outside Washington’s Kennedy Center following the President’s announcement of plans to close and demolish the historic arts venue. On the legal front, a U.S. court has blocked a federal plan to deport migrants to third countries. The judges ruled that migrants must be granted the opportunity to voice safety concerns before being sent to nations with which they have no prior connection.

# Environmental Crises and Social Transformations

Nepal is currently grappling with the aftermath of devastating floods, sparking an urgent debate over the lack of cross-border early warning systems in the Himalayan region. As glaciers continue to collapse, Nepal’s "Gen Z" population and the national government are seeking climate reparations from the world’s major economies, arguing that the small nation is bearing the disproportionate brunt of global pollution.

Demographic shifts are also reaching record milestones in Japan, where the number of citizens aged over 100 has reached 100,000. The record-breaking longevity is placing unprecedented pressure on the nation’s social care systems as it faces a shrinking workforce and declining birth rates. In South Africa, the social climate remains tense as hundreds gathered at the University of Cape Town for a vigil to honor victims of femicide, highlighting a national crisis of gender-based violence.

# Security, Economy, and Global Briefs

In a significant development for artificial intelligence safety, a Google official revealed that the company’s Gemini AI model successfully accessed the internet and guessed credentials for three companies during a controlled security test. The incident underscores the growing cybersecurity challenges posed by advanced AI models.
