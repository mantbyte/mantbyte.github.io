# India Hosts 18th BRICS Summit Amid Shifting Diplomatic Dynamics

New Delhi has taken center stage as world leaders converge for the 18th BRICS Summit, marking India’s fourth time assuming the chairmanship. Prime Minister Narendra Modi expressed optimism for "positive discussions with aspiration for global welfare" as the summit commenced. High-profile attendees include Russian President Vladimir Putin, who arrived in the capital early today for scheduled bilateral meetings with Prime Minister Modi.

The summit occurs against a backdrop of complex regional relations. While Chinese President Xi Jinping’s visit is viewed as a potential diplomatic thaw, analysts suggest that business ties remain constrained by mutual suspicion, regulatory hurdles, and visa restrictions. Simultaneously, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs in Dhaka clarified that Bangladesh Prime Minister Tarique Rahman was invited to the summit in his capacity as the Chair of BIMSTEC, rather than as a direct BRICS invitee. In other diplomatic engagements, Malaysian Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim is scheduled to meet religious and social leaders, including Kanthapuram A.P. Aboobacker Musliyar, on September 13.

# Legislative Shifts and Political Accountability Across States

State governments are facing increased scrutiny over administrative appointments and legislative reforms. The Jharkhand High Court has issued a strict two-month deadline for the state government to appoint a Chairman and members to the Jharkhand Public Service Commission (JPSC). This follows the arrest of former chairman L. Khiangte and the subsequent resignation of the entire panel amid protests over recruitment irregularities.

In West Bengal, the passage of a new inter-university transfer Bill has sparked debate. While the state government argues the Bill will optimize human resources in higher education, Opposition MLAs have flagged concerns regarding its implementation. Similarly, in the Telangana Assembly, the Monsoon Session opened with discussions on expanding IT hubs into tier-II cities, though the government noted that employment estimates remain contingent on formal corporate proposals.

On the national stage, the Congress party has criticized the Modi government for record delays in prorogation and called for an all-party meeting to build consensus on delimitation proposals. Meanwhile, in Bihar, Deputy CM Samrat Choudhary has reportedly rolled back at least six major decisions following significant pressure from the public and political opposition.

# Labor Unrest and Economic Policy Challenges

Banking services across India faced significant disruptions today as employees and officers launched a nationwide strike. Supported by the CPI(M), the protesters are demanding a five-day workweek and voicing opposition to the Union government’s policies aimed at privatizing the banking sector. Srinivasa Rao of the CPI(M) characterized the struggle as a defense of the public banking system against private interests.

Labor tensions are also rising in Chennai, where conservancy workers engaged under the National Urban Livelihoods Mission (NULM) continue to demand job permanency and fair wages despite recent government agreements. In Kerala, the state government is grappling with a power crisis; Electricity Minister Sunny Joseph defended the current administration, attributing the shortage to lapses during the previous tenure. Additionally, the Centre has urged Kerala to expedite its decision on the implementation of the PM SHRI school scheme.

# Scientific Innovation and Social Governance

The Hindu-VIT Biotech Conclave in Chennai highlighted India’s evolving role in global science. Suchitra Ella, co-founder of Bharat Biotech, emphasized the nation’s transition from a consumer of Western molecules to a leader in indigenous vaccine development. This shift toward self-reliance is seen as a cornerstone of India’s future scientific strategy.

In a notable case of administrative corruption, the Odisha vigilance department apprehended auditors for demanding bribes as low as ₹10 per student in government-run residential schools. Authorities seized over ₹14 lakh in cash during the investigation. 

In cultural news, tributes were paid to renowned Telugu writer Viswanatha Satyanarayana on his birth anniversary, with speakers praising his work as an embodiment of Indian culture. In the arts, the socio-political sports drama *Mandaadi*, centered on coastal sailboat racing, has received positive reviews for its performances and technical execution. Finally, the literary world saw the release of a long-awaited work regarding Sonia Gandhi’s "belonging," which has transitioned from being shelved to reaching public bookstores.
