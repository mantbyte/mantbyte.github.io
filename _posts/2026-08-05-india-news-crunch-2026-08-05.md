# National Daily Digest: Parliamentary Disruptions, Tamil Nadu Budget Dynamics, and Growing Student Protests

### Parliamentary Business, Big Tech Scrutiny, and Judicial Directives

The 13th day of the Monsoon Session of Parliament faced persistent disruptions, leading to the Lok Sabha being adjourned for the day amid continuous din. Despite the uproar, the Lower House passed the Bankers’ Books Evidence Bill without debate, while the Rajya Sabha initiated discussions on the Supreme Court Judges' Bill. Parliamentary Affairs Minister Kiren Rijiju criticized the Opposition, stating their primary target was to prevent the House from functioning effectively.

Simultaneously, the Parliamentary Standing Committee on Information Technology issued a stern warning to Meta, demanding an official apology for removing a video featuring the Prime Minister. The panel signaled that failure to comply could result in Meta losing its "safe harbour" immunity under the IT Act, which protects platforms from liability regarding user-generated content. Google India was also called out by the panel over rising financial frauds on its platform.

In the judicial sphere, the Supreme Court addressed the ongoing national student unrest concerning NEET-UG irregularities. A bench led by Chief Justice of India Surya Kant urged the government to adopt a persuasive approach rather than a heavy-handed response. Emphasizing that reasoning is the most effective resource, the Court stated that authorities must listen to and counsel Gen Z protesters rather than repress them.

### Tamil Nadu Budget Presentation and Political Friction Across States

Tamil Nadu's political landscape witnessed major developments following the presentation of the TVK-led government's maiden budget for 2026-27. Finance Minister Marie Wilson outlined key proposals, including the distribution of a one-sovereign gold coin for brides, while renewing calls for the Union government to abolish NEET and reinstate Class XII marks as the criterion for medical admissions. The Revised Budget Estimates projected the state’s outstanding debt at ₹10.98 lakh crore, with State’s Own Tax Revenue estimated at ₹2,26,740 crore. The State Assembly Speaker announced that the entire proceedings of the budget session, running through September 8, will be telecasted live.

The budget drew sharp criticism from Opposition leader and former Chief Minister M.K. Stalin, who dismissed it as a "waste" and a repackaging of older schemes, referring to it as a "remake of the Dravidian model." 

Tensions in Tamil Nadu escalated further with the brief detention of Opposition leader Udhayanidhi Stalin at the Sengipatti police station. Questioned for over an hour over alleged offensive remarks against an actor, Udhayanidhi denied any double entendre in his statements and accused the ruling administration of filing cases to divert public attention from pressing agrarian issues. Commentators noted the tactical similarities between the political maneuvers surrounding his arrest and historic state political rivalries. In a separate legal ruling affecting the state, the Madras High Court mandated that all retail TASMAC liquor outlets maintain bill books and issue receipts for every transaction.

In Uttar Pradesh, legislative proceedings were similarly disrupted as Chief Minister Yogi Adityanath condemned the Opposition's conduct as unconstitutional. Uttar Pradesh Assembly Speaker Satish Mahana expelled Samajwadi Party MLA Sachin Yadav for the remainder of the session after the lawmaker staged a protest displaying a photograph of the Chief Minister.

### Regional Controversies, Legal Verdicts, and Historical Markers

In Kerala, Chief Minister Pinarayi Vijayan defended his use of a helicopter and a scheduled meal during flood relief operations, characterizing the political criticism surrounding the event as unfortunate. Concurrently, Leader of the Opposition V.D. Satheesan faced questions regarding inconsistencies in his statements on the Vigilance and Anti-Corruption Bureau (VACB) findings in the Punarjani case, which he noted were conducted under the previous LDF administration.

In Jammu & Kashmir, the seventh anniversary of the abrogation of Article 370 was marked by street protests organized by the National Conference (NC) and the People's Democratic Party (PDP). Conversely, Lieutenant Governor Manoj Sinha described the 2019 abrogation as a "historic turning point" during an event where he distributed appointment letters to family members of terror victims.

Legal developments in Kerala saw the acquittal of all accused, including social activist M. Geethanandan, in a long-standing case linked to the Muthanga land agitation involving the murder of a police constable. 

Meanwhile, details emerged regarding the Ram Temple cash embezzlement case. An FIR alleges a coordinated theft operation involving temple personnel and cash-counting staff who systematically pilfered donations and concealed temple valuables during financial audits.

### Student Agitations over Competitive Examination Irregularities

Protests surrounding competitive examination irregularities in Jharkhand saw significant developments as student leader Devendra Nath Mahato ended his four-day hunger strike by accepting water at the personal request of environmental activist Sonam Wangchuk. Despite Mahato ending his fast, five additional student protesters joined the hunger strike to demand accountability for alleged irregularities in the JPSC and JSSC examinations. 

Extending support to the movement, Citizens for Justice and Peace (CJP) announced plans to send a delegation to Jharkhand. CJP representative Abijeet Dipke stated that their youth wing would act as a national pressure group to support students demanding transparency in public service examinations.
