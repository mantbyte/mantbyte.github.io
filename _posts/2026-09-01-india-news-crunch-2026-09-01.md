# National Security and Strategic Diplomacy

In a high-level address to the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO), Prime Minister Narendra Modi called for a unified global front to dismantle the "terror ecosystem." Emphasizing that there can be no place for "double standards" in dealing with terrorism, the Prime Minister urged member states to speak in a single voice against extremist threats.

Domestically, security agencies are on high alert following a significant breach at an Army establishment in Secunderabad. Authorities are investigating the theft of several firearms, including four AK-203 assault rifles, from a highly secured storage area. While police examine how suspects bypassed multiple security layers, local schools in the cantonment area have been advised to conduct classes online as a precautionary measure.

On the maritime front, the Indian Navy is advancing its deep-sea capabilities with the INS Nipun. The specialized vessel is designed for deep-sea diving, underwater intervention, and submarine rescue operations. Meanwhile, in Andhra Pradesh, police opened fire to apprehend a man who allegedly attacked a constable with a knife in the Krishna district. The suspect had reportedly attacked police personnel twice within 24 hours before attempting to escape.

# Economic Resilience and Governance Frameworks

Despite global volatility and surging Brent crude prices, India’s economy recorded a robust growth rate of 7.8%. Analytical reports suggest this growth was achieved even as the rupee faced pressure and international oil supply routes like the Strait of Hormuz saw disruptions. This resilience underscores the evolving structural strength of the Indian economy amid external shocks.

Preparations for the 2027 Census are also underway, marking a significant transition from a basic headcount to a comprehensive "data reservoir." Since 1951, the census process has evolved to capture intricate details regarding living conditions, education, and health for India’s projected 1.5 billion citizens.

In the corporate and regulatory sector, the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) has intervened in the financial proceedings of Subhash Chandra. A five-member bench stayed the approval of a ₹6.25-crore repayment plan and directed that no property be disposed of, directly or indirectly, while the proceedings remain pending. Additionally, the Tamil Nadu government has introduced a Bill to remove the requirement for a District Collector’s concurrence for wetland development in non-planning areas, a move that faced stiff opposition from the AIADMK, CPI, and CPI(M).

# Judicial Interventions and Civil Liberties

The Supreme Court has stepped in to protect journalistic freedom, barring the Uttar Pradesh Police from taking coercive action against journalist Abhishek Upadhyay. Upadhyay, who flagged alleged misappropriations regarding the Ram Temple, claimed that an FIR lodged against him in Ghaziabad was based on "concocted allegations" intended to harass him and unearth his sources.

Separately, the Supreme Court is examining the systemic failure of police officials to comply with directives to upload FIRs online within 24 hours. Legal experts have raised concerns that denying accused individuals a copy of the FIR violates statutory provisions. In another legal development, the Delhi Police have approached the apex court to invoke plenary powers under Article 142 to quash FIRs against NEET protesters, seeking a resolution to the ongoing friction surrounding the examination.

# Regional Updates and Environmental Challenges

Heavy monsoon rains have severely disrupted life across Kolkata and Southern Bengal, leading to widespread waterlogging and traffic congestion in low-lying areas. Similarly, in Gujarat, the state government has announced that schools affected by heavy rainfall may operate on holidays to complete the syllabus, following reports of students losing textbooks and missing critical instructional days.

In Tamil Nadu, Chief Minister M.K. Stalin oversaw the opening of the Mettur dam as water levels reached 91.56 feet against a full capacity of 120 feet. The focus now shifts to the longevity of water releases for agricultural needs. On the political front, the state saw heated rhetoric as Minister Aadhav Arjuna predicted a shift in the 2029 electoral scenario, asserting that the abolition of NEET would be a primary focus.

In Telangana, political tensions rose as the BRS criticized Chief Minister Revanth Reddy for making personal "bull-pig" comparisons regarding K. Chandrashekar Rao, alleging a decline in the quality of political discourse. Meanwhile, the resignation of IAS officer Divya Mittal has drawn public attention. The IIT-IIM graduate-turned-officer shared that her most enduring memory of service was the gratitude of an elderly woman seeing water arrive in her village, highlighting the human impact of administrative work.
