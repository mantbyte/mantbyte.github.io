# National Governance and Diplomatic Engagements

In a significant move to strengthen bilateral ties, External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar announced an extension of a $500 million line of credit to Guyana during a meeting with President Mohamed Irfaan Ali at the Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting. Addressing Commonwealth partners, Jaishankar emphasized the need to "de-risk and diversify" global supply chains, advocating for enhanced local distribution and the creation of more connectivity corridors to bolster business resilience.

On the domestic front, the Election Commission of India (ECI) has initiated a Special Summary Revision of electoral rolls across four states and two union territories, including Arunachal Pradesh, Mizoram, Odisha, Sikkim, Chandigarh, and Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu. The final rolls are scheduled for publication on January 21, 2025. Meanwhile, the Andhra Pradesh government has released the APTRT–2026 notification to fill 2,721 teacher posts, with applications opening on November 11. In Tamil Nadu, the state government has launched the ‘Vetri Special Vegetables Kit,’ offering ten varieties of produce for ₹100 to provide consumer relief through November 7.

# Judicial Oversight and Legal Mandates

The Supreme Court of India addressed allegations of police brutality during the July 20 protests, noting that "very few complaints" have been formally received thus far. However, the Court has granted petitioners the liberty to submit evidence, including video footage, to the High-Powered Enquiry Committee (HPEC) for further review. In a separate matter regarding educational equity, the Centre informed the Supreme Court that it is contemplating the appointment of a committee to review the University Grants Commission’s (UGC) equity rules.

In Kerala, the judiciary is navigating a high-profile case involving a fatal car crash in Cheranalloor allegedly caused by a juvenile driver. The Kerala High Court granted anticipatory bail to an Additional Judge whose son was involved, noting his cooperation with the investigation. Simultaneously, Judge Kavitha Gangadharan has sought data on the invocation of Section 105 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS) in accidents involving minors to determine the stringency of future bails. In a separate legal setback for student activists, the SFI’s plea against the suspension of students at a Sanskrit university was met with a refusal of relief from the Supreme Court; the organization has vowed to continue its "relay fast" until the disciplinary actions are revoked.

# Political Friction and Social Unrest

Political tensions escalated in Haryana as Union Home Minister Amit Shah, speaking at a rally in Karnal, accused Congress leader Rahul Gandhi of fostering a "ruckus culture" in Parliament and spreading anarchy. Shah’s remarks were part of the BJP's 'Loktantra Bachao, Arajakta Hatao' march, aimed at critiquing the opposition's parliamentary conduct.

In Karnataka, authorities detained sugarcane farmers to prevent a ‘Jail Bharo’ protest march toward Belagavi, citing prohibitory orders under Section 163 of the BNSS. In West Bengal, a dialogue opened between Christian representatives and senior RSS leader Jishnu Basu following reports of mob attacks on congregations. While Basu indicated that dialogue would continue, he noted that underlying issues remain to be addressed. Additionally, law enforcement in Malkajgiri dismantled an illegal loan racket that targeted vulnerable individuals, including an elderly doctor, using coercive tactics and hefty interest demands.

# Environmental Conservation and Public Safety

Conservationists have raised alarms over the ₹5.01 crore restoration project of the 17th-century Malshahi Masjid in Ganderbal, Kashmir. Built by Dara Shikoh, the mosque is considered a rare Mughal-era site; experts warn that improper restoration could erase its authentic historical layers. This call for eco-centric development was echoed by environmentalist Rajendra Singh, who criticized industrial projects in Amaravati and Eluru for alleged ecological violations.

Public safety concerns were highlighted by a tragic incident at Jharkhand’s Kathara colliery, where a coal pile collapse killed three people and injured three others; officials have characterized the incident as a consequence of coal theft. In Telangana, a survivor of a boat capsize in Bethupalli Peddacheruvu recounted a harrowing ordeal caused by a sudden surge in water levels, suspected to be triggered by an upstream irrigation tank breach. Furthermore, residents in Tamil Nadu’s Erode district have called for an urgent probe by the Pollution Control Board after the Cauvery river water turned green, raising suspicions of industrial contamination.
