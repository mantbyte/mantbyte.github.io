# Political Friction Intensifies Over Electoral Integrity and Demographic Data

The Indian political landscape is currently dominated by a sharp escalation in rhetoric between the opposition and the ruling government. Congress President Mallikarjun Kharge, speaking at a Congress Working Committee (CWC) meeting, has demanded the resignation of Prime Minister Narendra Modi and the removal of Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) Gyanesh Kumar. The demand stems from alleged irregularities in the electoral rolls. The Supreme Court has agreed to list a plea next week seeking the suspension of CEC Kumar, following an oral mention by senior advocates representing petitioner Rakesh Kumar Singh. Adding to the pressure, a former Home Secretary has urged the Opposition to boycott future elections until the CEC resigns.

Simultaneously, the Congress party has attacked the government’s High-Level Committee on Demographic Changes. Senior leader Jairam Ramesh characterized the panel’s pursuit of religion-wise data as a "patently diabolical agenda," alleging that the committee is operating with pre-conceived biases. In a separate address to the nation's youth, Rahul Gandhi urged citizens to protest against the government's perceived inaction on women's safety, asserting that the administration only responds under significant public pressure and criticizing the tendency to "punish women" for crimes committed against them.

# Judicial Oversight and Critical Public Health Findings

The Supreme Court of India has issued a stern rebuke to the Uttar Pradesh government, quashing the National Security Act (NSA) detention of an individual accused in the Sambhal violence. The top court ruled that the power of detention had been "illegally exercised" and slapped a ₹10 lakh cost on the state government. In another significant legal development, the Delhi High Court quashed a Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI) order that had directed Red Bull to remove the "energy drink" label from its products.

On the social front, a nationwide survey conducted by a Supreme Court-appointed National Task Force has revealed a burgeoning mental health crisis among students. The survey found that 15% of students felt overwhelmed or distressed, while 9% reported experiencing suicidal thoughts within the last six months. The final report is expected to be submitted by October 3. Meanwhile, the Madras High Court took a stand against "publicity litigation," imposing a ₹1 lakh fine on a lawyer who sought to recover bypoll expenses from resigned MLAs, noting that a similar case had already been dismissed on its merits.

# Diplomatic Relations and Global Trade Shifts

External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar has voiced concerns regarding the India-U.S. bilateral relationship, specifically citing a lack of understanding from Washington regarding India’s concerns over cross-border terrorism from Pakistan. Jaishankar noted that while ties are generally strengthening, the lack of full appreciation for these security threats impacts the depth of the partnership.

In a positive development for the pharmaceutical sector, the United States has waived tariffs on certain specialty drugs from 20 countries, including India. The zero-percent tariff rate will apply to drugs designated for rare diseases, infertility treatments, cell and gene therapies, and antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs). This move is expected to bolster India’s position as a global pharmaceutical hub and improve access to critical treatments.

# Regional Developments and Environmental Updates

In Kerala, local body bypolls across 38 wards in 12 districts have largely maintained the status quo. The United Democratic Front (UDF), Left Democratic Front (LDF), and the Twenty20 party successfully retained their respective seats in Ernakulam and Kozhikode, serving as a significant mid-term litmus test for the state's political climate.

In Andhra Pradesh, the flood situation in the Godavari region is beginning to stabilize as water levels recede at the Dowleswaram barrage. While relief operations continue in submerged tribal habitations in Chintoor, the inflow has fallen to 7.79 lakh cusecs, staying below the first warning level. In a separate regional update, Union Minister Pemmasani Chandrasekhar announced the release of ₹70.17 crore to 322 farmers for the Guntur Channel extension project.

Safety concerns were raised in Vizianagaram after firecrackers used to welcome the new BJP State chief, Madhav, sparked a fire at an apartment complex. While residents of Sai Kasturi Enclave were forced to evacuate due to smoke, no casualties were reported, and party leaders have promised to replace damaged equipment. Additionally, in Karnataka, Bescom has announced a second round of verification for 5.5 lakh Gruha Jyothi beneficiaries who have yet to submit the required documentation.
