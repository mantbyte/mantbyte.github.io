### Judicial Scrutiny and Legal Developments

The Supreme Court on Wednesday addressed the legal parameters governing public demonstrations while hearing a plea regarding the planned October 10 protest at New Delhi’s Jantar Mantar. The bench observed that holding protests without requisite permission is not in accordance with the law, as the court sought clarity on clearances sought by Citizens for Justice and Peace (CJP) and associated Left outfits. In response, organizers argued that Jantar Mantar remains a designated protest venue requiring no formal sanction. However, the Delhi Police formally denied permission for the gathering, citing past violations of undertakings, while opposition groups alleged preemptive detentions of their supporters.

In another key ruling, the Supreme Court stayed a High Court bail order in a ₹3,000-crore narcotics case, noting that the "mask and face must face the same consequences" before directing the accused back to prison. On affirmative action, the apex court upheld an Andhra Pradesh High Court judgment regarding Backward Classes (BC) quotas, finding the decision reasonable and reaffirming that breach of the 50% reservation ceiling cannot proceed in the absence of supporting empirical data. Concerning the electoral revision process in West Bengal, the court proposed that judicial tribunals first hear disenfranchised voters seeking re-inclusion in the voter roll before addressing petitions seeking exclusions.

At the district and state levels, a Delhi court granted former Wrestling Federation of India chief Brij Bhushan Sharan Singh time to respond to a petition challenging his acquittal; the public prosecutor requested 15 days to confirm whether the Delhi Police would pursue an appeal. In Tamil Nadu, former DMK Minister E.V. Velu and his son E.V. Kumaran approached the Madras High Court for anticipatory bail in an illegal lake bed mining case. The court granted the Directorate of Vigilance and Anti-Corruption (DVAC) until October 13 to submit its written response.

### Electoral Shifts, Governance, and Regional Affairs

By-election results across multiple states highlighted changing political fortunes. The Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) expanded its margin in West Bengal, while the newly launched Tamizhaga Vettri Kazhagam (TVK) established an early lead in Tamil Nadu. In Puducherry, the ruling All India N.R. Congress (AINRC), led by Chief Minister N. Rangasamy, emerged victorious.

In infrastructure and administrative developments, the upcoming Jewar International Airport in Gautam Buddha Nagar, Uttar Pradesh, is set to be officially named the Narendra Modi International Airport. 

State-level governance saw escalated tensions in Kerala, where Thiruvananthapuram Mayor V.V. Rajesh asserted that the opposition United Democratic Front (UDF) and the Left Democratic Front (LDF) are operating in concert to destabilize the BJP-led municipal administration through unfounded allegations. The Kerala Legislative Assembly is also preparing for a nine-day session commencing October 12, during which lawmakers will review two key legislative measures: the Kerala Court Fees and Suits Valuation (Amendment) Bill, 2026, and the Madras Hindu Religious and Charitable Endowments (Amendment) Bill, 2026.

In Karnataka's Shivamogga district, the village of Harigi implemented a community ban on *beegaroota*—traditional post-wedding non-vegetarian banquets—to alleviate financial stress on agrarian households amid a regional drought. Meanwhile, Hyderabad’s entertainment sector announced that Comic Con 2026 will be held at Bharat Future City from December 11 to 13, featuring the Indie Game Utsav and cosplay exhibitions, despite logistical questions raised by attendees over the distant venue.

### Diplomatic Engagements and International Developments

India’s Ministry of External Affairs (MEA) issued a firm response to the United States' decision to suspend certain corporations from the PERM labor certification green card pathway. Clarifying that PERM remains distinct from the temporary H-1B visa category, the MEA stated that unilateral restrictions do not align with shared bilateral ambitions, while dismissing remarks by U.S. Vice-President J.D. Vance as "unwarranted." Concurrently, Tata Consultancy Services (TCS) confirmed that the regulatory move would not affect its operations, detailing plans to recruit an additional 15,000 U.S. workers over the coming five years to expand its domestic talent base.

In neighborhood diplomacy, New Delhi finalized the appointment of senior career diplomat and MEA Secretary (Economic Relations) Sudhakar Dalela as India’s next ambassador to Nepal, ending speculation concerning a potential political appointee to the Kathmandu mission.

In West Asia, aviation operations and regional security were disrupted following a Houthi drone and missile strike targeting the international airport in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia. The attack resulted in three fatalities, including an aircraft pilot, and inflicted structural damage on a commercial Saudia aircraft.
