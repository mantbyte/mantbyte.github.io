# Mantbyte Daily Digest: National News and Events

## Natural Disasters and Public Safety

Odisha’s coastal districts faced severe weather conditions as Cyclone Akvan made landfall. The India Meteorological Department (IMD) reported that the landfall process commenced around 8 AM and concluded by 11:20 AM, with the Bhubaneswar Meteorological Centre noting that the duration spanned several hours. Despite rough seas and strong winds, no major damage has been reported. In response to the cyclone, the Odisha Assembly was adjourned until the morning of November 23. 

Meanwhile, Tamil Nadu’s Director of Geology and Mining has issued a directive to all district collectors mandating the fencing of all abandoned quarries. The order follows recent reports of fatal accidents involving unprotected sites. In wildlife updates, the Kerala Forest Department intends to tranquilize, radio-collar, and release the wild tusker 'Chakkakompan' in Chinnakkanal, altering previous relocation plans following complaints from NGOs. Additionally, the tiger known as 'Explorer' has reached the Mylavaram forest near Vijayawada, prompting a safety alert across four villages while forest officials track its movements via GPS.

## Judiciary and Legal Developments

The Supreme Court issued a limited, one-time arrangement permitting the lighting of a lamp on the ‘Deepathoon’ at Thirupparankundram for Karthigai Deepam on November 24. A Bench led by Justice Aravind Kumar stayed the Madras High Court’s conclusive findings from a January 6 judgment that had declared the practice an essential religious right. 

In other legal matters, the Supreme Court dismissed two pleas concerning Cauvery water sharing, with Deputy Chief Minister D.K. Shivakumar noting acute drinking water shortages following the relief sought. The apex court also upheld the Karnataka High Court order allowing bike taxi operations, dismissing the state government's challenge. Furthermore, the Supreme Court rejected a Public Interest Litigation (PIL) that sought a database of individuals filing repeated complaints of sexual offences, citing serious risks regarding the potential disclosure of victims' identities.

## Governance, Policy, and Regional Updates

The Kerala Food and Civil Supplies Department has sanctioned the procurement of 25,000 tonnes of rice from the Food Corporation of India (FCI) at a cost of ₹59.75 crore for market intervention this December. In Andhra Pradesh, an agricultural workers’ union has demanded lease payments and plots for assigned landowners in Amaravati, pointing out that nearly 1,800 acres of assigned land—primarily belonging to Dalit and weaker sections—were acquired for the capital. 

Political and administrative developments drew attention elsewhere as the Delhi government decided to open the former Chief Minister's residence, known as the 'Sheesh Mahal', to the public. In Tamil Nadu, the Madras High Court dismissed as withdrawn a plea filed by former Minister C. Vijayabaskar in a gutkha case after judges advised against clubbing two distinct prayers together.
