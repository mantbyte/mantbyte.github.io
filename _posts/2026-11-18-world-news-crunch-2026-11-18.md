# Mantbyte Daily Digest: Global News and World Events

### International Diplomacy, Trade, and Geopolitics
President Trump has arrived in China for the APEC summit in Shenzhen—a tropical city prepared for the event with extensive surveillance and preparation—though analysts anticipate few concrete breakthroughs or outcomes from the talks given the rivalry between the two nations. On the sidelines of APEC, Chinese President Xi Jinping pitched free trade, positioning Beijing as a provider of a stable and predictable environment amid global trade disruptions. Elsewhere in Asia, Prince William's visit to India underscores a growing partnership between New Delhi and London, which continues to advance despite Modi’s "decolonisation" rhetoric and the nations' colonial history. Meanwhile, a Times analysis reveals that Beijing is actively running a concerted campaign to paint Japan as an aggressor and resurgent military threat.

### Global Conflicts and Regional Security
Geopolitical analysts warn that a new kind of world war is already underway through "piecemeal wars," where separate regional conflicts are increasingly linked by the interests of rival powers. In the Middle East, shipping vessels face heightened risks transiting the Hormuz Strait due to limited U.S. protection, with the Atlantic Council noting that the U.S. Navy cannot safeguard every passing vessel. In domestic political shifts, Democrat Mary Peltola secured a U.S. Senate win in Alaska, giving Democrats 56 Senate seats and establishing a buffer in Republican states ahead of the 2028 elections. In diplomatic developments involving past leadership, President Trump briefly met with old friend Imelda Marcos in Manila, where the Philippine leader and host treated Trump to a late-night order of Jollibee fried chicken.

### Domestic Crises, Protests, and Human Rights
In Haiti, thousands of citizens took to the streets to celebrate and rally in support of Salvadoran troops and President Nayib Bukele, praising the military deployment for fighting gangs in their country. Meanwhile, public outrage has erupted in Ghana over the government's purchase of a new $60-million presidential jet as citizens struggle with a rising cost of living. In Southeast Asia, Thai authorities are scrambling to provide safe drinking water to villagers after arsenic from Myanmar mines contaminated the Mekong River. Rohingya refugees living in nearly a decade of exile are now pinning their hopes for a return home on an upcoming International Court of Justice (ICJ) genocide verdict. In the United States, Venezuelan boxer Linares, 41, was arrested by immigration authorities at El Paso International Airport and faces deportation, while a forensic expert analysis of autopsy records and photos suggests that Jeffrey Epstein’s 2019 death was more likely a homicide than a suicide.

### Technology, Legal, and Cultural Developments
U.S. Congresswoman Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez and TV personality Paris Hilton have spoken out against AI-generated deepfakes that specifically target women and girls. Concurrently, technological experts have highlighted how artificial intelligence is exposing system vulnerabilities, sparking a dangerous rush to patch digital infrastructure. In sports and regulatory news, Cricket Australia announced it will establish a forum—likely led by a former judge—to address player relations following previous controversies like "Sandpapergate," though the organization has ruled out opening any new probes or issuing new sanctions.
