# Daily Digest: Infrastructure Milestones, Political Developments, and Regional Updates Across India

## Infrastructure and Regional Development

Progress is being recorded on key transport and infrastructure projects across the country. In Karnataka, the vital four-laning of the Mangaluru-Bengaluru National Highway 75 in Dakshina Kannada and Hassan is nearing completion, with the entire route now four-laned except for the 26 km Shiradi Ghat stretch between Maranahalli and Addahole, though the project has faced a decade-long timeline and doubled costs. Meanwhile, international rail connectivity is set to expand with the introduction of the Ayodhya Cantt-Janakpur Dham Saryu-Kamala Express, a weekly India-Nepal train featuring 10 stations and scheduled departures every Monday.

In urban mobility, the Cyberabad Moves 2026 event highlighted pressing urban transport challenges and public transit gaps through interactive visual exhibits. In Mysuru, the Forum of Organizations and Associations of Mysuru (FOAM) has proposed a four-point plan to the Centre to revitalize the local tourism sector, calling for a convention centre, heritage conservation, evening cultural programs, and expanded UDAN and direct flight connectivity. In Jammu and Kashmir, the Border Roads Organisation (BRO) dismissed social media claims regarding a viral video of an earthmover near Sheshnag Lake, clarifying that the activity is strictly restricted to widening and maintaining existing paths.

## National and Regional Politics

Political developments dominated headlines as parties prepare for upcoming electoral battles and address governance issues. In Tamil Nadu, AIADMK general secretary Edappadi Palaniswami announced statewide protests scheduled for December 2 against the government, citing corruption and governance irregularities. Concurrently, the Madras High Court nominated DGP Venkataraman to monitor the Directorate of Vigilance and Anti-Corruption (DVAC) probe into the Tamil Nadu public libraries book-sharing scam. 

In Andhra Pradesh, vote counting is underway for local body polls involving 964 wards across 23 urban local bodies, drawing heavy voter turnout and triggering exchanged allegations between the TDP and YSRCP. In Telangana, Chief Minister Revanth Reddy accused Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Amit Shah of conspiring to remove voter names to secure a fourth term, speaking during the Samvidhan Raksha March in Hyderabad. At the same time, Telangana Minister Uttam Kumar Reddy noted at a CII conclave that the state's defence and aerospace sector has outpaced IT and pharma growth, positioning the region as a major global manufacturing hub.

At the national level, Congress chief Mallikarjun Kharge addressed Constitution Day events, asserting that Uttar Pradesh taught the BJP a lesson in the 2024 Lok Sabha polls and blunted the '400 paar' narrative. Kharge also urged party member Imran Masood to prioritize action over aggressive rhetoric amid seat-sharing frictions with the Samajwadi Party. In Maharashtra, NCP leader Supriya Sule dismissed rumors concerning a potential alliance with the BJP-led NDA, emphasizing her focus on parliamentary duties and the people of Baramati.

## Governance, Law, and Media

The judiciary and administrative bodies have stepped up oversight on public order, media content, and internal operations. The Supreme Court has issued notices to the Centre and leaders in response to a public interest plea seeking uniform guidelines and standard operating procedures to govern protests nationwide. The apex court is also scheduled to hear contempt petitions concerning the Thirupparankundram lamp-lighting controversy.

In digital media and broadcasting, the central government issued a formal warning to Netflix, reminding the streaming platform that the 2025 advisory enforcing a strict ban on Pakistan-origin content remains fully in effect. In railway administration, the North Central Railway issued a strict warning to staff prohibiting the creation of social media reels while on duty, citing severe risks to the safety of train operations. 

Finally, agricultural updates indicate that severe drought conditions in Karnataka have heavily impacted groundnut cultivation, restricting sowing to 1.59 lakh hectares against a target of 2.54 lakh hectares and triggering widespread crop failures, resulting in supply shortages and rising market prices.
