# Mantbyte Daily Digest: Parliamentary Showdowns, Judicial Concerns, and Regional Developments

## Legislative Agenda and Political Alignments
The Centre has announced a special sitting of Parliament beginning November 30, following more than 100 days since both Houses were adjourned without prorogation. Ahead of the session, the Union Cabinet has cleared an amended Delimitation Bill, which proposes a 50% increase in seats for all States while maintaining the present ratio of seats among States until 2051. The Bill is expected to be tabled during the upcoming special sitting, a move following a previous attempt that was rejected in the Lok Sabha during the Monsoon session in April. 

Political maneuvering is already underway in the capital. Telugu Desam Party leader N. Chandrababu Naidu has arrived in Delhi to attend a National Democratic Alliance (NDA) meeting regarding the developments. Meanwhile, the Shiv Sena—led by Maharashtra Deputy Chief Minister Eknath Shinde and boasting 13 Lok Sabha and two Rajya Sabha MPs—has issued a whip directing all its MPs to be present for the special sitting. 

## Judiciary, Governance, and Electoral Controversies
Former Chief Justice of India B.R. Gavai has written to President Droupadi Murmu, alleging a “complete disregard” for merit and inclusivity in judicial appointments. The former CJI stated that recent Collegium recommendations to constitutional courts favour “one particular section” at the exclusion of marginalised communities.

In Tamil Nadu, the Supreme Court has intervened in the Thirupparankundram controversy, ordering a deepam to be lit at the 'deepathoon' on November 28 while suspending the Board of Trustees. The apex court declared that authorities “wilfully disobeyed” previous court orders and issued show-cause notices on contempt against the Tamil Nadu Chief Secretary, DGP, HR&CE Commissioner, and the Board of Trustees. 

Meanwhile, electoral roll discrepancies have emerged in Karnataka. An investigation into the final Special Intensive Revision (SIR) rolls revealed over 17,000 entries where parents are listed as younger than or the same age as their children, alongside thousands of other entries reflecting an age gap of less than 15 years.

## Climate Pressures and Resource Crisis in Kerala
Kerala is facing severe environmental and resource challenges following a prolonged rain deficit. According to India Meteorological Department (IMD) data, nearly 70% of the State remained significantly rain-deficient at the end of the southwest monsoon season on September 30, marking it as the 7th-lowest southwest monsoon season on record. While the IMD had issued heavy rainfall alerts during the monsoon's onset, the broader season remained subdued.

The compounding failure of both the southwest and northeast monsoons has triggered severe drought concerns. Disaster management assessments indicate that 27 rainfall stations have crossed the Trigger-1 drought threshold, setting the stage for a significant reduction in water availability and early summer-like conditions. Paradoxically, the weakening of easterly winds and resulting atmospheric stability have simultaneously caused temperatures to dip to single digits in the high ranges, ushering in an early winter chill. Analysts note that these weather anomalies, combined with historical comparisons to past El Niño years, raise questions regarding the long-term management of the State's power sector and water resources.

## Regional Administration and Public Welfare
In other regional developments, political tensions escalated in Kerala as CPI(M) leader P. Rajeeve urged the police to register a case against the Chief Minister following remarks made at a Youth Congress event, where threats were allegedly directed at political and student activists. In Tamil Nadu, traffic was heavily disrupted on the Outer Ring Road near Avadi after a truck transporting liquor from a Tasmac warehouse in Kakkalur to Red Hills overturned.

In welfare policy, the Punjab Chief Minister launched the Smart Ration Card Scheme aimed at benefiting 10 lakh beneficiaries. The scheme guarantees five kg of free wheat per family member per month upon completion of e-KYC, highlighting ongoing state-level expansions of public distribution systems amid broader national debates concerning the fiscal sustainability of perpetual cash-transfer and subsidy schemes.
